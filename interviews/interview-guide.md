# Interview guide

> **DRAFT. Not agreed by the team.** A 10 to 15 minute script. Change any question that does not sound like you.

Ground rules (Mom Test style):

- Ask about what they did last time, not what they would do someday. Past behavior is evidence. Opinions about the future mostly are not.
- Do not pitch Nook until the last part. If you describe the idea early, people answer to be nice.
- Ask for numbers and stories: when, what, how much, what happened next.
- Do not fill silences. Let them keep talking.
- Write down their words as close as you can. Put your own reading in a separate section of the note (see [template.md](template.md)).

## Before you start (1 minute)

Consent line, read out loud:

> "We are a student team in a UW Bothell business class, and we are learning how people deal with furniture when they move. This takes about 10 to 15 minutes. We will take notes but will not record you, and we will not write your name down. We will call you something like 'Interviewee 3'. You can skip any question or stop at any time. Is that OK?"

If they say no, thank them and stop. Do not take notes.

## Screener (1 minute)

1. "Have you moved in the last 12 months, or will you move in the next 12 months?"
   - Neither: thank them and stop. Do not log the interview in the tally.
   - Either: continue. Note which one (or both).
2. "Where do you live right now: UW housing, an off-campus rental as a student, or a rental and you are not a student?"
   - Gives the segment: on-campus, off-campus, or non-student renter.

## The last move (6 to 8 minutes)

3. "Tell me about the last time you moved. When was it, and where from and to?"
4. "What big things did you bring with you, and what did you have to buy for the new place?"
5. "How did you decide what would fit in the new room? Did you see the room before move-in day?"
6. "Did you measure anything beforehand? With what?"
7. "Did anything not fit, or nearly not fit? Walk me through what happened."
   - If yes: "What did you do then? What did it cost you, in money or time?"
   - Mark `last_move_fit_problem` as yes only if they describe a real case, not "it could happen".
8. "What did the whole move cost you, roughly, with the van, movers, new furniture and so on?"
9. "Since then, have you moved big furniture around again, for example to rearrange a room?"

## The idea and the interest measures (3 to 4 minutes)

Now describe Nook in one or two plain sentences, for example:

> "We are working on a phone tool that lets you see whether furniture fits a room before you move it or buy it. It is a student project and nothing is for sale yet."

Then ask these three in this order, and write the answers exactly:

10. **Early list (yes/no):** "We are starting an early list for when there is something to try. Would you like to be on it?"
    - If yes, point them to the landing page sign-up. Do not write their email in this repository.
11. **Price (yes/maybe/no):** "If it had saved you the trouble on your last move, would $14.99 for that one move have been worth it to you?"
    - Then: "Why?" Their reason matters more than the answer.
12. **Interest (1 to 5):** "On a scale of 1 to 5, where 1 is 'would not use it' and 5 is 'I needed this last time', where are you?"

## Close (1 minute)

13. "Is there anyone else who just moved or is about to move who we should talk to?"
14. "Thank you."

Then, within the hour, fill in a note from [template.md](template.md) and add one row to [tally.csv](tally.csv).
